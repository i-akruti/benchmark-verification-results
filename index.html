06 / ACCEPTANCE THRESHOLDS

Ranking is not
permission to approve.

We tested a three-way policy: automatically accept low-scored cases, escalate the middle, and reject high-scored cases. The lower threshold targeted 80% defect recall; that target itself permits 20% of defects to be automatically accepted.

Interpreting the test: Jev and GPT-5.4 fell short of the preset 80% recall target on the threshold-evaluation half. The other three systems met it, but that target still permits defective automatic acceptances. Only Decider enabled any auto-rejections on the final split.

The final half was reserved for the specific threshold evaluation, but was also used in other analyses of the dataset. This is not an independent deployment validation. Repeated-split summaries are excluded here pending a code correction.

07 / METHODOLOGY

What was actually measured

Five models were evaluated against labels established by outside annotators. The site displays aggregate results rather than private logs or full source code.

01

MMLU-Redux 2.0

995 items: all 338 defects and 657 valid questions randomly sampled within 57 subjects. Horvitz–Thompson weights correct for uneven subject/class inclusion probabilities. The population contains 5,668 usable items.

02

SWE-bench annotations

300 tasks: 150 passed the annotation-quality filter, 150 did not. Labels derive from three professional reviewers' severity judgments; severity 2–3 denotes a serious issue. Population weighting reflects 1,699 annotated tasks.

03

Model comparisons

All systems received the same task state and category definitions. AUROC comes from returned probabilities or LLM confidence judgments. Comparisons use paired bootstrap intervals, with subject/repository-cluster checks.

04

Review-budget estimates

Cases are ordered by defect score. A chosen percentage of weighted population mass enters the hypothetical review queue. Counts measure prioritization, assuming perfect human verification of selected defects.

Additional methodological limits +

Possible model exposure. Both benchmark datasets and their labels are public and may have appeared in training data.

Label uncertainty. Human annotations are reference judgments, not infallible truth. SWE-bench annotators accessed broader code context than the models.

Scope. Neither dataset contains newly AI-generated benchmarks. No human-review sessions ran; no time savings were measured.

Comparisons. Published intervals are pointwise, exploratory comparisons; repeated hypothesis testing was not corrected across all contrasts.

Display data. The graphs read directly from the aggregated CSV-derived figures distributed with this site. Raw API logs and full task texts are not published here.
